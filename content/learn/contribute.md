This library exists because reliable, plain-language information about blood
donation in India is harder to find than it should be. Most of what a worried
family finds online is either a hospital marketing page or a copy of a copy, and
very little of it says who wrote it or when anyone last checked it.

We are building the opposite of that, and we need people who actually know the
subject.

## Who we are looking for

**Haematologists and transfusion medicine specialists**, to review clinical
pages before they publish. This is the role we need most. Nothing clinical goes
live on this site without a named reviewer, which means several pages are written,
complete, and sitting unpublished.

**Blood bank officers and technicians**, for the operational reality — what
actually happens to a unit, where the delays come from, what goes wrong on a camp
day and how a well-run blood bank prevents it. This knowledge is almost entirely
undocumented and it is the most useful thing on the site when it is written down.

**Doctors who treat transfusion-dependent patients** — thalassaemia, sickle cell
disease, haemophilia, oncology. The pages a patient's family needs are about
living with a lifelong need for blood, and they should be written by people who
have that conversation every week.

**Medical writers and translators.** We publish in English and Marathi, and we
want Hindi. A good translation of a clinical page is not a mechanical task: the
words have to be the ones a patient's family actually uses, and a wrong word in a
clinical context is a safety problem, not a style problem.

**Patients and donors.** If you live with a transfusion-dependent condition, or
you have organised camps, or you have donated for years, you know where the
published advice is wrong or useless. That is worth more than another summary of
the same material.

## What we publish

Pages that are useful to someone who is not a clinician, and that say plainly
who wrote them and when they were last checked. Every page in this library carries
its author, its reviewer, its publication date, its last review date and its
sources, visibly, on the page.

We do not publish anything that reads as an advertisement, anything that cannot
cite a source, or anything that promises a medical outcome. We do not publish
sponsored content of any kind, and we do not accept payment for placement or
links.

The [editorial policy](/learn/editorial-policy) sets out the standards in full,
including how corrections are handled and what happens when a reviewer and the
foundation disagree.

## What we pay, and what you get

We are a small not-for-profit, and we are honest about this: we cannot currently
pay professional rates for clinical writing or review. What we offer is a visible
byline with your name, credentials and affiliation on every page you write or
review, permanent attribution in the page's structured data, and work that is
genuinely read by patients' families rather than filed.

If you would want to be paid and we cannot, please tell us anyway. We would
rather know what the work is worth and look for funding for it than quietly
publish less.

## How to get in touch

Write to **contact@choudhari.ngo** with:

- which area you could write about or review
- your qualifications and current affiliation, and your registration number if
  you are a registered medical practitioner
- whether you want to author, review, or both

For review work we verify medical registration before your name appears on a
page, because a byline that cannot be checked is worth nothing to the reader it
is meant to reassure.

You do not need to send a sample or a proposal. A paragraph saying what you do
and what you could help with is enough to start.

## Corrections

If something on this site is wrong, tell us — you do not need to be a clinician
to do that, and you do not need to be polite about it. Write to
**contact@choudhari.ngo** with the page and what is wrong with it.

We correct errors on the page itself rather than quietly editing them away, and a
page that has been corrected says so. A library that does not admit its mistakes
is not a library.
